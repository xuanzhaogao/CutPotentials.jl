Accuracy, cost and limitations

Other checks:

  • Laplacian. $\operatorname{tr} H + a\rho < 10^{-9} \max|H|$ everywhere off the surfaces, and one-sided on them (with the $a$ of that side). The Hessian components are never overwritten using this identity.
  • coef. It equals $a$ off the surfaces to $10^{-10}$, and $(a_A + a_B)/2$ on flat faces.
  • Equal coefficients. With all $a = a_0$, the result is exactly $a_0$ times the smooth field.
  • Merging. The merged, the union-box and the region-by-region (unmerged) descriptions of two equal boxes agree to 1e-10.

Where the remaining Hessian errors come from:

  • The 2e-10 off-surface (exact fields) comes from the reference. At 1e-8 from a face it violates its own trace identity by the same amount, while the computed Hessian satisfies it to 1e-16.
  • The 4.5e-10 on faces is a target 1e-3 from an edge. Its extrapolation error is $(\delta/\ell)^4$ with $\delta = 5 \times 10^{-6}$ and $\ell = 10^{-3}$. At 1e-2 from the edge it is 7e-14.

At $h = 0.05$ (2.8M samples), SmoothField takes 7.4 s and the other steps are unchanged.

evaluate runs one DxU FMM for $W$, two for the potential and six for the gradient. The Hessian adds twelve FxdU FMMs, so the layer cost grows linearly in the number of densities.

Limitations

  • Hessian at edges and corners. It is singular there (logarithmically, at box edges). The on-surface one-sided limit needs hess_offset to be well below the distance $\ell$ to the nearest edge, with error about $(\delta/\ell)^4$. Off the surface, targets 1e-6 from an edge are accurate (2e-11).
  • On-surface Hessian targets must be flagged with side. For curved surfaces, a point of the exact surface lies within rounding of the discretized one, on either side. $U$ and $\nabla U$ do not care; the Hessian needs the flag, and hess_offset must exceed the geometric error of the elements.
  • Discretized geometry. The regions are those bounded by the discretized surfaces: SCTL's polynomial interpolants of the patches. Curved surfaces therefore carry a geometric error, set by order and the element size.
  • Closedness is not checked. The interfaces must make up closed region boundaries, with consistent orientation and $\delta a$. Maue's identity, and the cancellation of free terms into $W$, rely on it.
  • Per-target-set setup. The near corrections depend on the targets. Many targets close to large surfaces cost memory: about 1 GB per 1e7 near-pair nonzeros, three times that for the Hessian kernel.
  • The density must be resolved by its samples and decayed at the edge of the sample box, which is the TKM assumption. The error is about $|\hat\rho(\pi/h)|$, $e^{-\sigma^2\pi^2/2h^2}$ for a Gaussian, plus the mass the box leaves out. That is $10^{-10}$ at $h = \sigma/2$ and $10^{-3}$ at $h = \sigma$. The surface corrections remove only the error that screening adds, not under-resolution. See Examples. Surface nodes and targets outside the padded box use point charges, which are accurate there.
  • Grading parameters. The default L0 and support are heuristics; give the density's width and support when known. The default support, density_support(src) (samples above $10^{-10} \max\rho$), is conservative. Surfaces inside it get uniform elements of size $\kappa L_0$. A tighter support, such as the density's centre for a Gaussian, grades them with the same accuracy and fewer elements (see Examples). Elements whose patches do not resolve the geometry need max_size or more patches (nsub).
  • Platform. The package is MPI-free and Float64 only. ParticleFMM sums directly below 40,000 targets (SCTL's choice).
