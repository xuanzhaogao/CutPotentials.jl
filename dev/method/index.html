Method

Spectral (TKM / NUFFT) or point-charge evaluation of the screened samples aρ sees the jumps of aρ. It converges only like O(h) to O(h²), which measured as 1e-2 to 1e-1 relative errors. CutPotentials.jl instead keeps every volume computation on the smooth, unscreened ρ, and moves the jumps into surface integrals of known smooth data.

Smooth fields

φ0 = N[ρ], ψ = ∇φ0 and H = ∇ψ come from SmoothField:

  • Inside the padded source box: the truncated-kernel method. A type-1 NUFFT (FINUFFT) of the samples is multiplied by the truncated Laplace kernel 2(sin(Lk/2)/k)² and placed on one real scalar fine grid. φ0, ψ and H are then gathered from that grid with the window and its first two derivatives, w, w′ and w″. The window is an analytic ES window tapered by (1 − z²)³.
  • Elsewhere: point charges, summed by SCTL's ParticleFMM (Laplace3D_FxU, _FxdU, _Fxd2U).

Identities

For a region Ω with outward normal n, write S[q] = ∫Γ G q dS and D[q] = ∫Γ ∂{nx}G q dS. Green's third identity gives:

N[χ ρ]   = χ φ0 + D[φ0] − S[∂_n φ0]
∇N[χ ρ]  = χ ψ  + D[ψ]  − S[H n − tr(H) n]                    (Δφ0 = −ρ)
∇∂_i N[χ ρ] = χ H e_i + ∇×S[n × H e_i] − ∇S[(H n)_i − tr(H) n_i]

How the Hessian identity is derived:

  1. Differentiate the gradient identity.
  2. The term ∇D[ψi] is hypersingular. Replace it by Maue's identity, ∇D[f] = ∇×S[n × ∇Γ f], which holds on closed surfaces. Here n × ∇Γ ψi = n × H e_i is known pointwise.
  3. The Hessian then needs only single-layer gradients of densities built from H. It needs no third derivatives, no ∇ρ and no differentiation on panels. These integrals are near-singular off the surface.

Checks.

  • The jumps reproduce [∇∇N[χρ]] = −ρ n nᵀ.
  • The identity is verified numerically to 1e-14 with exact fields (tests).

Merging the regions. Write U = a0 φ0 + Σj (aj − a0) N[χ_j ρ] and store each interface once:

  • its normal n_m points from side A to side B;
  • its coefficient is δam = aA − a_B.

Shared faces then combine, and faces with δa = 0 drop out. The free term Σj (aj − a0) χj(y) is written as −W(y), with W = Σm δam Dm[1] computed by the same quadrature:

U   = (a0 − W) φ0 + Σ_m δa_m (D_m[φ0] − S_m[ψ·n])
∇U  = (a0 − W) ψ  + Σ_m δa_m (D_m[ψ] − S_m[Hn − tr(H) n])
∇∇U = (a0 − W) H  + Σ_m δa_m (∇×S_m[n × H e_i] − ∇S_m[(Hn)_i − tr(H) n_i])_i   (symmetrized)

On-surface targets

U and ∇U equal a0 φ0 + Σ δa D[f − f(y)] − …. Their double layers have densities that vanish at the target, so they are continuous through every interface, edge and junction.

  • No snapping needed. A target exactly on a surface, or 1e-16 off it, gets the same value. The free term is automatically (aA + aB)/2 on a face and Σ αj aj at a junction.
  • Rounding. This replaces the face-plane snapping that the research code needed. Box face coordinates are still merged within rounding, so that neighbouring boxes share exact rectangles.

coef = a0 − W is returned as a diagnostic.

The Hessian jumps by −δa ρ n nᵀ across an interface.

  • Flagged targets. For a target flagged with a side vector ν, the package returns the one-sided limit on the side ν points to. It is extrapolated from y + kδν̂ for k = 1..4 (H ≈ 4H₁ − 6H₂ + 4H₃ − H₄, with δ = hess_offset, default 1e-5·L0).
  • Unflagged targets are evaluated where they are. A target exactly in the plane of a flat face gets the two-sided average.

Discretization

  • Surface elements. The surface integrals use their own SCTL Gauss–Legendre elements (QuadElemList, order 12 by default). Each patch is split until its size is at most κ(d + L0), where d is the distance to the density's support (κ = 0.5).
  • Nodal data. ψ and H are evaluated pointwise at the nodes.
  • Far field. The node rule is applied by ParticleFMM: DxU for the dipoles, FxU for the charges and FxdU for the single-layer gradients.
  • Near and singular pairs. SCTL's near_correction handles them, for the kernels Laplace3D_DxU, _FxU and _FxdU. It resolves targets at any distance, down to 1e-12, as one-sided limits, to about 1e-14.
