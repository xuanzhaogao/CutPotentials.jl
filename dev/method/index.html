Method

Spectral (TKM / NUFFT) or point-charge evaluation of the screened samples $a\rho$ sees the jumps of $a\rho$. It converges only like $O(h)$ to $O(h^2)$, which measured as $10^{-2}$ to $10^{-1}$ relative errors. CutPotentials.jl instead keeps every volume computation on the smooth, unscreened $\rho$, and moves the jumps into surface integrals of known smooth data.

The target quantity is

\[U(y) = \int G(y, x)\, a(x)\, \rho(x)\, dx, \qquad G(y, x) = \frac{1}{4\pi |y - x|},\]

with $a = a_j$ on the regions $\Omega_j$ and $a = a_0$ outside, together with $\nabla U$ and $\nabla\nabla U$. Write $N[f] = \int G f\, dx$ for the Newton potential.

Smooth fields

The smooth fields $\varphi_0 = N[\rho]$, $\psi = \nabla \varphi_0$ and $H = \nabla \psi$ come from SmoothField:

  • Inside the padded source box: the truncated-kernel method. A type-1 NUFFT (FINUFFT) of the samples is multiplied by the truncated Laplace kernel $2\,(\sin(Lk/2)/k)^2$ and placed on one real scalar fine grid. $\varphi_0$, $\psi$ and $H$ are then gathered from that grid with the window and its first two derivatives, $w$, $w'$ and $w''$. The window is an analytic ES window tapered by $(1 - z^2)^3$.
  • Elsewhere: point charges, summed by SCTL's ParticleFMM (Laplace3D_FxU, _FxdU, _Fxd2U).

Identities

For a region $\Omega$ with indicator $\chi$, boundary $\Gamma$ and outward normal $n$, write

\[S[q](y) = \int_\Gamma G(y, x)\, q(x)\, dS_x, \qquad D[q](y) = \int_\Gamma \partial_{n_x} G(y, x)\, q(x)\, dS_x .\]

Green's third identity, with $\Delta \varphi_0 = -\rho$, gives:

\[\begin{aligned} N[\chi\rho] &= \chi\, \varphi_0 + D[\varphi_0] - S[\partial_n \varphi_0], \\ \nabla N[\chi\rho] &= \chi\, \psi + D[\psi] - S\big[H n - \operatorname{tr}(H)\, n\big], \\ \nabla \partial_i N[\chi\rho] &= \chi\, H e_i + \nabla\times S[n \times H e_i] - \nabla S\big[(H n)_i - \operatorname{tr}(H)\, n_i\big]. \end{aligned}\]

How the Hessian identity is derived:

  1. Differentiate the gradient identity.
  2. The term $\nabla D[\psi_i]$ is hypersingular. Replace it by Maue's identity, $\nabla D[f] = \nabla\times S[n \times \nabla_\Gamma f]$, which holds on closed surfaces. Here $n \times \nabla_\Gamma \psi_i = n \times H e_i$ is known pointwise.
  3. The Hessian then needs only single-layer gradients of densities built from $H$. It needs no third derivatives, no $\nabla\rho$ and no differentiation on panels. These integrals are near-singular off the surface.

Checks.

  • The jumps reproduce $[\nabla\nabla N[\chi\rho]] = -\rho\, n n^\mathsf{T}$.
  • The identity is verified numerically to $10^{-14}$ with exact fields (tests).

Merging the regions. Write

\[U = a_0\, \varphi_0 + \sum_j (a_j - a_0)\, N[\chi_j \rho]\]

and store each interface $m$ once:

  • its normal $n_m$ points from side A to side B;
  • its coefficient is $\delta a_m = a_A - a_B$.

Shared faces then combine, and faces with $\delta a = 0$ drop out. The free term $\sum_j (a_j - a_0)\, \chi_j(y)$ is written as $-W(y)$, with $W = \sum_m \delta a_m D_m[1]$ computed by the same quadrature. The Hessian line below is symmetrized:

\[\begin{aligned} U &= (a_0 - W)\, \varphi_0 + \sum_m \delta a_m \big(D_m[\varphi_0] - S_m[\psi \cdot n]\big), \\ \nabla U &= (a_0 - W)\, \psi + \sum_m \delta a_m \big(D_m[\psi] - S_m[H n - \operatorname{tr}(H)\, n]\big), \\ \partial_i \nabla U &= (a_0 - W)\, H e_i + \sum_m \delta a_m \big(\nabla\times S_m[n \times H e_i] - \nabla S_m[(H n)_i - \operatorname{tr}(H)\, n_i]\big). \end{aligned}\]

On-surface targets

$U$ and $\nabla U$ equal $a_0 \varphi_0 + \sum \delta a\, D[f - f(y)] - \dots$. Their double layers have densities that vanish at the target, so they are continuous through every interface, edge and junction.

  • No snapping needed. A target exactly on a surface, or $10^{-16}$ off it, gets the same value. The free term is automatically $(a_A + a_B)/2$ on a face and $\sum_j \alpha_j a_j$ at a junction, with $\alpha_j$ the solid-angle fractions.
  • Rounding. This replaces the face-plane snapping that the research code needed. Box face coordinates are still merged within rounding, so that neighbouring boxes share exact rectangles.

The free term $a_0 - W$ is returned as coef, a diagnostic.

The Hessian jumps by $-\delta a\, \rho\, n n^\mathsf{T}$ across an interface.

  • Flagged targets. For a target flagged with a side vector $\nu$, the package returns the one-sided limit on the side $\nu$ points to. It is extrapolated from $H_k = \nabla\nabla U(y + k\delta\hat\nu)$, $k = 1, \dots, 4$:

    \[\nabla\nabla U(y^\pm) \approx 4H_1 - 6H_2 + 4H_3 - H_4,\]

    with $\delta$ = hess_offset, default $10^{-5} L_0$.

  • Unflagged targets are evaluated where they are. A target exactly in the plane of a flat face gets the two-sided average.

Discretization

  • Surface elements. The surface integrals use their own SCTL Gauss–Legendre elements (QuadElemList, order 12 by default). Each patch is split until its size is at most $\kappa (d + L_0)$, where $d$ is the distance to the density's support ($\kappa = 0.5$).
  • Nodal data. $\psi$ and $H$ are evaluated pointwise at the nodes.
  • Far field. The node rule is applied by ParticleFMM: DxU for the dipoles, FxU for the charges and FxdU for the single-layer gradients.
  • Near and singular pairs. SCTL's near_correction handles them, for the kernels Laplace3D_DxU, _FxU and _FxdU. It resolves targets at any distance, down to $10^{-12}$, as one-sided limits, to about $10^{-14}$.
